# The AI De-Slopifier

**Use AI to help humans create interesting content — not more AI slop.**

Give an AI a few notes and ask it to write a newsletter and it can turn one interesting idea into 800 words of polished nothing.

Repetition. Generic business language. Empty conclusions. Manufactured significance. Forced narratives. Familiar rhetorical structures appearing again and again.

The AI De-Slopifier is an editorial framework designed to prevent that.

It helps people develop and edit content while preserving the ideas, experiences and point of view that made it worth writing in the first place.

## Two ways to use it

### Create

Start with an idea, experience, argument or rough notes.

Write freely, or ask the De-Slopifier to help develop the idea through questions.

It will:

1. Establish what you're trying to say.
2. Identify important information that may be missing.
3. Ask only the questions it needs.
4. Present its understanding of the story or argument back to you.
5. Wait for you to confirm or correct it.
6. Create the content.
7. Audit the draft for slop.
8. Revise and check it again before presenting it.

The AI does not get to decide what you meant.

### De-Slopify

Already have a draft?

Give it to the De-Slopifier. It doesn't matter whether you wrote it yourself, wrote it with AI, or generated it entirely with AI.

Before rewriting, it identifies the substance of the piece and checks whether anything important is missing. If it needs information, it asks rather than filling the gap itself.

Once the intended narrative is confirmed, it checks for:

- repetition;
- generic language;
- empty conclusions;
- manufactured insights or significance;
- forced narratives;
- formulaic rhetorical structures;
- abstraction replacing useful information;
- loss of the author's voice;
- unsupported assumptions or additions;
- unnecessary length.

It then edits the piece and audits its own rewrite.

## The Narrative Checkpoint

One of the easiest ways for AI to produce bad content is to misunderstand the author's point and then write confidently around that misunderstanding.

Before writing or substantially rewriting anything, the De-Slopifier therefore presents the bones of the story or argument back to the author.

For example:

> **Before I write, let me check I've understood you correctly:**
>
> - Your team spent three months building a feature customers had repeatedly requested.
> - After launch, hardly anyone used it.
> - Talking to customers afterwards revealed that the original problem was real, but the requested feature wasn't actually a good solution to it.
> - Your point isn't that teams should ignore customer requests. It's that requests need to be understood in the context of the underlying problem.
> - You want to use the experience to discuss how you approach product discovery now.
>
> Is that the argument you want to make?

If that's wrong, correct it before anything gets written.

The De-Slopifier should never turn its interpretation of your experience into your opinion.

## The Content Readiness Check

The De-Slopifier maintains an internal checklist of what it needs to understand before writing.

Depending on the content, this can include:

- purpose;
- audience;
- substance;
- the author's perspective;
- useful specifics or examples;
- why the reader might care;
- desired outcome;
- voice;
- relevant boundaries.

These are not mandatory questions for the author.

If the information is already present, the De-Slopifier should use it. If one important piece is missing, it should ask about that piece rather than making the user complete a questionnaire.

Not every piece needs every element. A personal story doesn't necessarily need a call to action. A technical explanation doesn't necessarily need a personal anecdote.

The question is simply whether there is enough genuine material to write the piece without the AI filling important gaps itself.

## The Slop Audit

Every generated or edited piece goes through an editorial review.

The audit looks for questions such as:

**Am I repeating the same idea in different words?**

**Does this sentence actually say anything?**

**Could this paragraph disappear without the reader losing anything?**

**Have I replaced something specific with abstract language?**

**Have I created a conclusion or significance that the author never expressed?**

**Have I forced the material into a familiar narrative structure?**

**Am I using rhetorical patterns because they sound good rather than because they help communicate the idea?**

**Did I introduce facts, motivations, emotions or interpretations that weren't provided?**

**Does this still sound like the author?**

After revising, the De-Slopifier checks again.

Because an AI attempting to remove AI slop is perfectly capable of replacing it with different AI slop.

## The principle

The De-Slopifier separates thinking from writing.

It establishes what the author actually wants to say before producing polished prose. When something important is missing, it asks.

It doesn't require every experience to contain a lesson, every setback to produce personal growth, or every observation to reveal a wider truth.

Sometimes an interesting story is just an interesting story.

## What this is not

The AI De-Slopifier is not an AI detector or a tool for making AI-generated text "undetectable."

Nor is it a blacklist of supposedly AI words.

There is nothing inherently wrong with rhetorical questions, three-part sentences or constructions such as "it's not X, it's Y."

The problem is using rhetorical technique as a substitute for thought.

The De-Slopifier evaluates these patterns in context.

The core specification is platform-independent.

Implementations are planned for:

- Gemini

Each will follow the same editorial process while adapting to the capabilities of the platform.
